## 4. Household Records, Roles, and Access

### 4.1 Account Holder

Every Household Record on the Platform has exactly one Primary Account Holder (the "Account Holder"), who controls it: its data, who has access, invitations, designations, and settings.

### 4.2 Trusted Participants

The Account Holder may invite other people ("Trusted Participants") to their Household Record, in one of two roles:

- **Manager:** Manages the whole Household Record on the Account Holder's behalf. Managers always see the whole household.
- **Shared Access:** Sees only the Collections (which may be narrowed to certain categories or properties) and Vault documents that are shared with them. Each share has its own access level: **Viewer** (can see), **Contributor** (can also add and edit, but not remove), or **Full Access** (can also remove). People with Shared Access cannot share onward, invite others, or manage designations, and cannot add new Vault documents.

Shared Access never extends to the whole household. The Account Holder or a Manager may invite people and share Vault documents; only the Account Holder can change a Trusted Participant's role or the Collections shared with them, or remove them. Changes take effect immediately.

### 4.3 Collections and Beneficiaries

Items within a Household Record are organized into Collections. The Account Holder may name Beneficiaries for the household, and may name different Beneficiaries for a Collection; if a Collection has none of its own, the household's Beneficiaries apply.

### 4.4 Intentions

For each item, you may record an Intention (Keep, Sell, Gift, Donate, or Undecided) and who it is Intended For. If no one is named for an item, the Beneficiaries of its Collection or household apply. Intentions and Beneficiaries record your wishes inside HeirDock. Like designations (Section 5), they have no independent legal force and are not a substitute for a will or other legal instrument.

### 4.5 Your Responsibilities as Account Holder

If you are the Account Holder, you are responsible for: managing who has access to your Household Record; setting appropriate roles and access levels for Trusted Participants; managing designations (see Section 5); controlling Professional access (see Section 7); and ensuring that the information you provide is accurate and that you have the right to share it.

### 4.6 Your Responsibilities as a Trusted Participant

If you are a Trusted Participant, you agree to: use your access only for the purposes the Account Holder has authorized; not share your sign-in details with others; not attempt to access data outside what has been shared with you; and respect the confidentiality of the Household Record and its contents.

A designee may be a person (with or without a HeirDock account), a trust, or a Professional's firm. A designation takes effect only once it has been accepted: either the designee accepts it in HeirDock, or the Account Holder records that the designee has agreed. The Account Holder or a Manager may revoke a designation at any time before it takes effect in a life event.

### 5.2 Steward and Alternate Steward

The Account Holder or a Manager may designate one Steward, who will look after the Household Record after the Account Holder's passing, and one Alternate Steward. If the Steward cannot serve, HeirDock Support works with the Alternate Steward. Until Stewardship begins (Section 6), a Steward has no added access and keeps their regular role, if any.

### 5.3 Care Steward

The Account Holder or a Manager may designate one Care Steward, who will look after the Household Record in Care Mode: when the Account Holder cannot manage things themselves, or chooses to hand over for a time.

In Care Mode, the Care Steward becomes a Manager with access to the whole Household Record and acts on the Account Holder's behalf, including for billing: the Care Steward may update the payment method and cancel the subscription. The Account Holder keeps view-only access during Care Mode. The Care Steward cannot change who the Account Holder is, or report the Account Holder's passing. Billing actions and other significant changes the Care Steward makes are recorded in the Household Record's activity log, which the Account Holder can see. If the subscription is canceled, we also email the person who pays for it.

Care Mode has no fixed end date. It ends when the Care Steward returns the Household Record to Active, or when HeirDock Support does so at the Account Holder's request. Where Care Mode began because the Account Holder could not manage things themselves, HeirDock may first ask the Account Holder to confirm their identity and that they are able to manage the household again.

If a Care Steward resigns, can no longer act, or does not respond to HeirDock Support, HeirDock Support may act for the Household Record, and will let the Account Holder know.

## 6. Life Event Transitions

### 6.1 Household Modes

Each Household Record is in one of four modes:

- **Active:** The Account Holder manages the Household Record. This is the default mode.
- **Stewardship:** The Account Holder has passed away. The designated Steward manages the Household Record.
- **Care Mode:** The Account Holder cannot manage things themselves, or has chosen to hand over for a time. The designated Care Steward manages the Household Record.
- **Archived:** The Household Record is closed and can no longer be changed. A Household Record is archived at the end of Stewardship, or when a subscription has lapsed as described in Section 9.5.

### 6.2 Reporting a Life Event

The Account Holder or the Care Steward may report in the Platform that the Account Holder can no longer manage the household, or has chosen to hand it over. To tell us that an Account Holder has passed away, please contact HeirDock Support at [legal@heirdock.com](mailto:legal@heirdock.com).

### 6.3 Review and Documentation

Care Mode begins as soon as it is reported. HeirDock Support may review it afterwards, may ask for documents (such as a Power of Attorney), and may reverse it. To report a passing, contact HeirDock Support, who may ask you for documents such as a death certificate; Stewardship begins only after HeirDock Support has confirmed the Account Holder's passing. Our review is an administrative process and is not a legal determination of the validity of any document.

### 6.4 Stewardship

When Stewardship begins, the designated Steward becomes a Manager with access to the whole Household Record. Other Trusted Participants keep their existing access, and Professionals keep their existing consent-based access.

Stewardship is one-way: a Household Record in Stewardship can only move on to Archived. It cannot return to Active.

### 6.5 Care Mode

In Care Mode, the Care Steward becomes a Manager with access to the whole Household Record and acts on the Account Holder's behalf, as described in Section 5.3. The Account Holder remains the Account Holder. When Care Mode ends, the Household Record returns to Active, and the Care Steward returns to the access they had before Care Mode.

### 6.6 A Quieter Experience

During Stewardship and Care Mode, the Platform hides marketing content. During Stewardship, it also hides most suggestions and recommendations.

### 6.7 Limitations of Life Event Features

HeirDock provides life event features as a convenience to help families manage access to Household Records during difficult times. You acknowledge and agree that:

- HeirDock is not a legal, estate planning, or fiduciary service.
- Life event transitions are subject to our review. We may delay, decline, or reverse a transition if we have reason to believe the reported event is inaccurate or the request is not properly authorized.
- HeirDock is not liable for any disputes between Trusted Participants, Stewards, Care Stewards, Beneficiaries, family members, or other parties regarding access to or control of a Household Record.
- You are solely responsible for maintaining appropriate legal instruments (wills, trusts, powers of attorney) outside of the Platform.
- HeirDock does not adjudicate competing claims to a Household Record. In the event of a dispute, we may suspend the Household Record pending resolution or a court order.

## 10. Audit Trail and Data Integrity

### 10.1 Immutable Event Log

The Platform maintains an immutable audit trail of all household mode transitions (life event transitions). Each event in the log includes: the originating and target modes, the user who initiated the transition, any notes, a timestamp, and a SHA-256 cryptographic checksum linking the event to the previous event in the chain.

### 10.2 Data Snapshots

At each mode transition, the Platform captures a comprehensive snapshot of the Household Record's state at that point in time. Snapshots are stored with their own SHA-256 hash for integrity verification.

### 10.3 Chain Integrity Verification

HeirDock can verify the integrity of a Household Record's mode change event chain at any time. If you would like us to verify yours, contact [legal@heirdock.com](mailto:legal@heirdock.com).

### 10.4 Limitations

The audit trail and data snapshots are provided as data integrity features. While they are designed to be tamper-evident, they are not a legal record of ownership, transfer, or intent. HeirDock does not warrant that the audit trail satisfies the evidentiary or record-keeping requirements of any specific jurisdiction or legal proceeding.
